# Locale annex: Australian English

Apply this annex on top of the shared humanizer body when `resolver.humanizer_locales` includes `au`. This annex outranks the body on spelling, vocabulary, units, money and register.

Australian English follows British conventions with a short list of local exceptions. The exceptions are the part that gets missed, so learn them rather than defaulting to British forms across the board. New Zealand clients follow the same spelling rules; the vocabulary, tax and seasonal notes below are Australian and need checking before reuse.

## Australian spelling

**-ise, not -ize:** organise, recognise, realise, prioritise, customise, optimise, finalise, categorise, emphasise, summarise, maximise, minimise, visualise, specialise, apologise, standardise, merchandise. Also the noun forms: organisation, optimisation, categorisation. This is the single most common Americanism to slip through. Sweep for -ize every time.

**-yse, not -yze:** analyse, paralyse, catalyse. The noun stays analysis.

**-our, not -or:** colour, honour, favour, behaviour, neighbour, labour, humour, endeavour, flavour, vapour, rumour, harbour. Note the suffix exceptions that drop the u: honorary, humorous, laborious, vigorous.

**-re, not -er:** centre, theatre, fibre, metre, litre, calibre, spectre. The American form is correct only inside a proper noun registered that way, for example a US brand trading under its own spelling.

**-ence and -ce for nouns:** defence, offence, pretence, licence as the noun and license as the verb, practice as the noun and practise as the verb. "A best practice approach" but "we practise what we preach".

**Double l before a suffix:** travelled, travelling, labelled, modelled, cancelled, fuelled, levelled, marvelled, signalling, counsellor, enrolled.

**-ement, not -ment:** judgement, acknowledgement. Legal writing often prefers judgment for a court decision. Both are accepted in Australia; hold one choice through the piece.

**Other standard Australian forms:**
- fulfil, fulfilled, fulfilment. Single l in the base word
- dialogue, catalogue, analogue. Keep the -ue
- cheque for a payment, check for verifying
- enrol, enrolled, enrolment
- instil, instalment
- storey for a floor of a building, story for a narrative. Commercial and retail writing uses both, so watch this one
- towards, afterwards, forwards. Keep the s
- learnt and spelt are both acceptable alongside learned and spelled
- ageing, not aging
- sizeable, likeable, liveable. Keep the e
- grey, not gray

**Australian departures from British spelling:**
- program for everything: software, television, a schedule of events, a funded initiative. Programme is rare in Australia and reads dated. This is the sharpest divergence from British usage, where programme is still standard for non-software senses
- jail, not gaol, in all modern usage. Gaol survives only in heritage names
- inquiry and enquiry are both current, with inquiry preferred for formal investigations
- Government and institutional style guides lean towards minimal punctuation and sentence case headings more strongly than British house styles do

If unsure whether a spelling is Australian or American, default to the British form. The exceptions are short and listed above.

Shortened forms such as arvo, servo, rego, ute and smoko are genuine Australian register but belong in speech and internal notes only. Never use them in client-facing documents; they read as forced when written by anyone the reader does not already know.

Never write "reach out" in Australian business copy. Name the action instead: "I'll call on Thursday."

## Units, dates and money

**Units:** metric always. Square metres, kilometres, kilograms, centimetres, litres, Celsius. Never square feet unless quoting a US source directly, in which case give the original figure and mark it. Hectares for land. Metric holds in property, retail, construction and freight alike, with no imperial survivals of the British kind.

**Dates:** day before month. The full form is D Month YYYY. In numerals, DD/MM/YYYY. Never MM/DD/YYYY, never March 9th. Financial quarters are written Q1 to Q4 against the financial year, not the calendar year, so say which.

**Time:** twelve hour with am and pm in conversational copy, twenty-four hour in schedules. Always name the state or the time zone abbreviation, because the eastern states, central and western differ and only some observe daylight saving. Never write a national meeting time without a zone.

**Currency:** the dollar sign means AUD by default. Mark every other currency explicitly, especially USD and NZD. Write large figures consistently: $1.2m or $1.2 million.

**Financial year:** 1 July to 30 June. A two digit FY number refers to the year the period ends in, not the year it starts, so a financial year that closes on 30 June is named for that closing year. Never assume the calendar year is the reporting year.

**Tax:** GST is 10 per cent and is usually quoted inclusive in consumer prices. Be explicit about ex GST or inc GST in every commercial figure. Payroll tax is set by state and thresholds differ, so name the state rather than generalising.

**Seasons:** reversed. Christmas trade is peak summer trading, the school year starts in late January, the winter slowdown falls in the middle of the calendar year, and end of financial year sales land in June. Never import northern hemisphere seasonal logic into Australian retail, tourism or agricultural writing. Watch for "spring campaign" language copied from a northern source and landing six months out.

## Australian business register

Australian business writing is flatter and more sceptical than American business writing. Hype reads as a warning sign, not as confidence. This matters more than any spelling rule.

- Understate. "It worked well" carries more weight here than "it was a phenomenal success."
- No motivational register. American business writing rewards inspiration; Australian business writing treats it as a sales tactic.
- Self-deprecation is a credibility move, not a weakness. There is room for it in most contexts.
- Dry humour is welcome in internal and industry writing. Keep it out of anything legal or formal.
- Never open with "I hope this email finds you well." Get to the point.
- Directness is politeness. Padding a request with three softening clauses reads as evasive rather than respectful. This is where Australian register parts company with British indirectness: soften less, and say the actual thing.
- Avoid the American superlative habit. Incredible, amazing, best in the business. If the work was good, describe what it did.
- Titles are used lightly. First names from first contact in almost every sector, including at executive level.
- Sign off with Thanks, Cheers, or Regards. Cheers is normal in business email and reads warm rather than casual.
- Tall poppy sensitivity is real. Claiming market leadership invites the reader to look for the hole in the claim, so back it or drop it.

**Acknowledgement of Country:** where a piece is a formal presentation, an event, or a public-facing document where one is appropriate, flag it rather than writing one. Getting this wrong is worse than not including it. Ask which Country the wording should name, and whether the client already has approved wording of their own.

**Regulatory caution:** consumer law here is strict on misleading claims, including comparative pricing, environmental claims and testimonials. Flag any claim that would need substantiating rather than writing it in and hoping.

## Australian final sweep

Run these three passes after the shared final check. They catch the errors that slip through most often for this locale.

1. **Search for -ize and -yze,** then for -or where -our belongs (color, honor, behavior, favor), then for -er where -re belongs (center, theater, meter), then for "toward" without the s. These four families slip through most often.
2. **Read for hype.** Anything that would make an Australian reader think "settle down" gets cut or flattened. Check the opening line and the closing line first; that is where inflation collects.
3. **Check the seasons, the financial year and the GST basis.** Any seasonal reference imported from a northern source will be six months wrong, any bare year reference may mean the calendar year rather than the financial year, and any price without an ex GST or inc GST marker will generate a query.
