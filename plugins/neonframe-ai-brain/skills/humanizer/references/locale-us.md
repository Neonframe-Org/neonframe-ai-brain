# Locale annex: American English

Apply this annex on top of the shared humanizer body when `resolver.humanizer_locales` includes `us`. This annex outranks the body on spelling, vocabulary, units, money and register.

American English is a full set of conventions, not the absence of conventions. Enforce it as deliberately as any other locale. Most drift into American text comes from British or Australian forms carried in from source material, quotes, or a previous draft written for another audience.

## American spelling

**-ize, not -ise:** organize, recognize, realize, prioritize, customize, optimize, finalize, categorize, emphasize, summarize, maximize, minimize, visualize, specialize, apologize, standardize, memorialize. Also the noun forms: organization, optimization, categorization.

**Genuine -ise exceptions,** which stay -ise in American English too: advertise, advise, arise, compromise, comprise, despise, devise, exercise, improvise, revise, supervise, surprise, televise, franchise, merchandise (verb).

**-yze, not -yse:** analyze, paralyze, catalyze. The noun stays analysis.

**-or, not -our:** color, honor, favor, behavior, neighbor, labor, humor, endeavor, flavor, vapor, rumor, harbor, savor. Also favorite, honorable, behavioral.

**-er, not -re:** center, theater, fiber, meter, liter, caliber, specter. Keep the -re form only inside a proper noun registered that way, for example an arts venue trading as "Theatre".

**-ense, not -ence:** defense, offense, pretense, license as both noun and verb. There is no noun/verb split in American English the way there is elsewhere.

**Single l before a suffix:** traveled, traveling, canceled, canceling, labeled, modeled, fueled, leveled, marveled, signaling, counselor, counseling, jeweler. Enrollment and installment take the double l because the base word doubles: enroll, install.

**-ment, not -ement:** judgment, acknowledgment, abridgment.

**Other standard American forms:**
- program, never programme, in every sense: software, television, grant funding, a schedule of events
- fulfill, fulfilled, fulfillment
- practice for both noun and verb. Never practise
- catalog, dialog in software contexts, analog. Dialogue survives in the sense of conversation and is safe there. Catalogue reads British
- check for a written payment order, never cheque
- instill, distill
- story for a floor of a building as well as for a narrative. Never storey
- toward, afterward, forward, backward, downward. Drop the trailing s
- learned and spelled, never learnt or spelt
- aging, not ageing
- sizable, likable, livable, salable. Drop the e
- gray, not grey, in general prose
- inquiry covers both formal investigations and ordinary questions. Enquiry reads British
- jail, not gaol
- skeptical, not sceptical
- tire on a wheel, curb on a street, ax as a tool, plow in a field, mold on bread, draft for both an early version and a current of air
- while, not whilst. Among, not amongst. Amid, not amidst

When unsure, default to the shortest and simplest form. American orthography usually took the shorter route.

## Units, dates and money

**Units:** US customary in general business, consumer and marketing writing. Square feet for floor area, miles for distance, pounds and ounces for weight, gallons for volume, Fahrenheit for temperature, inches and feet for dimensions. Use metric in scientific, medical, pharmaceutical, engineering and military contexts, where it is the working standard. Never convert a quoted metric figure silently; give the original and the conversion in parentheses if the reader needs both.

**Dates:** month before day. Write the full form as Month D, YYYY. In numerals, MM/DD/YYYY. Never DD/MM/YYYY, which will be misread. Avoid ordinal suffixes in formal writing: write March 9, not March 9th, except when the day stands alone in speech-like copy.

**Time:** twelve hour clock with a.m. and p.m. Include the time zone for anything scheduled across states, and use the abbreviations that shift with daylight saving rather than a fixed offset.

**Currency:** the dollar sign means USD by default. Mark every other currency explicitly, including CAD and AUD, because the symbol collides. Write large figures as $1.2 million or $1.2M consistently, and use commas as thousands separators with a period for decimals.

**Tax:** sales tax is set by state and often by county and city on top, so no single national rate exists. Consumer prices are quoted before tax, and tax is added at the register. Never state a national sales tax rate. For business to business figures, say whether tax is included. There is no VAT and no GST; do not import either word.

**Fiscal year:** the federal government runs October to September. Companies vary, so name the period rather than assuming: "the fiscal year ending in June" is safer than "FY" alone. Quarters run Q1 to Q4 and are widely used in earnings language.

**Seasons:** northern hemisphere. Back to school lands in late summer, peak retail trade runs from late November through December in cold weather, and summer is the slow season in many business categories.

## American business register

American business writing rewards clarity, confidence and forward motion. Directness is expected and reads as respect for the reader's time. Optimism is the default register rather than a sales tactic.

- Lead with the result, then the reasoning. American readers scan the first line and decide whether to continue.
- Confidence is permitted. A clear claim backed by a number reads as credible, not boastful. Superlatives without evidence still read as hype, so anchor every strong claim to a figure or an example.
- Enthusiasm is acceptable in a way it is not in British or Australian writing. Keep it earned, not performed. One genuine "this worked" beats three "incredible" claims.
- Use first names from the first contact in most sectors. Formal titles persist in law, medicine, government, academia and parts of finance; follow the counterpart's own signature.
- Keep openings short. A first name, then the update in the same line, works fine. Skip the throat-clearing pleasantries entirely in internal mail.
- Ask directly. Softening a request with three conditional clauses reads as unsure, not polite. Name what you need and when you need it.
- Bullets, headers and bolded takeaways are normal in American internal documents. Keep them out of email and social copy, where they read as machine output.
- Do not import British understatement. "Not bad" reads as lukewarm to an American reader, and "we may wish to consider" reads as having no opinion.
- Do not import Australian flatness or self-deprecation as a credibility move. It reads as low confidence rather than as honesty.
- Sign off with Best, Thanks, or Best regards. "Kind regards" reads faintly foreign.
- Legal and regulatory caution is real. Avoid guarantees of outcome, medical or financial claims, and comparative claims about named competitors unless the client has approved the wording.

## American final sweep

Run these three passes after the shared final check. They catch the errors that slip through most often for this locale.

1. **Search for -ise and -isation.** Organise, prioritise, recognise, optimisation. Then search for -our (colour, honour, behaviour, favour) and -re (centre, theatre, metre). These four families account for most of the drift.
2. **Check date order and the dollar sign.** Any date written as day before month will be misread by an American reader, and any bare dollar figure sourced from another market needs marking or converting.
3. **Check "toward", "while" and "among" have no trailing s or archaic form.** Towards, whilst and amongst are the three function words that survive a British source draft undetected. While sweeping, confirm no metric unit has been left in consumer-facing copy.
